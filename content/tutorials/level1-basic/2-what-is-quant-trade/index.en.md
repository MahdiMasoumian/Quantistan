### Quantitative Finance

Quantitative Finance is an interdisciplinary field of finance that applies mathematics, statistics, data science, programming, and computational methods to analyze financial problems. Its primary objective is to model financial phenomena, measure risk, value financial assets, analyze market behavior, and develop data-driven approaches to financial decision-making. Many of the concepts and technologies used in modern financial markets—including risk management, financial instrument pricing, portfolio optimization, algorithmic trading, and quantitative trading—are built upon the foundations of quantitative finance.

### Quantitative Trading

Quantitative Trading is an approach to designing and developing trading strategies in which investment decisions are driven by data, mathematical models, statistical methods, and other quantitative techniques. Rather than relying on intuition, emotions, or subjective judgment, this approach uses historical market data and analytical tools to identify patterns and trading opportunities.

The primary goal of quantitative trading is to develop strategies whose rules can be clearly defined, tested, and evaluated. As a result, the strategy development process typically begins with collecting and analyzing market data. Trading hypotheses are then formulated based on quantitative evidence and evaluated using historical data. Only when the results prove satisfactory is a strategy considered ready for deployment in live markets.

One of the defining characteristics of quantitative trading is its emphasis on evidence-based decision-making. Every component of a trading strategy—from entry and exit rules to risk management and capital allocation—should be designed and evaluated using measurable results. Consequently, quantitative traders employ a wide range of techniques, including statistics, probability theory, optimization, machine learning, and other data analysis methods to develop and improve trading strategies.

### Machine Learning in Quantitative Trading

One of the most important data-driven approaches in quantitative trading is the use of machine learning algorithms. By leveraging large volumes of financial data, these algorithms can identify patterns and relationships that are difficult—or in some cases impossible—to detect using traditional methods. As a result, machine learning has become an important area of both academic research and practical application in quantitative trading. In the following lessons, we will examine how these algorithms can be applied step by step to design, evaluate, and develop trading systems.

### Summary

Quantitative Finance provides a scientific framework for analyzing financial problems using quantitative methods, while Quantitative Trading represents one of its most important practical applications. In this approach, trading decisions are driven by data and supported by tools such as statistics, mathematical models, and machine learning.
