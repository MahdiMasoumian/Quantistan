### What Is a Financial Market?

A **financial market** is an environment where individuals, businesses, governments, and other institutions buy, sell, or exchange financial assets.

Simply put, any place where buyers and sellers can trade financial assets is considered a financial market.

The primary purpose of financial markets is to transfer capital from those who have available funds to those who need financing. In addition, financial markets facilitate price discovery, provide liquidity, and help market participants manage risk.

Today, most transactions are carried out through electronic trading systems, and many financial markets operate either continuously or during designated trading hours.

---

### Why Are Financial Markets Important?

Without financial markets, buying and selling assets would be significantly more difficult, time-consuming, and costly. Financial markets make it possible to:

* Connect buyers and sellers efficiently.
* Determine asset prices through supply and demand.
* Channel capital toward productive economic activities.
* Provide opportunities for investing and trading.
* Help companies and governments raise capital.

For these reasons, financial markets are considered one of the fundamental pillars of every modern economy.

---

### What Assets Are Traded in Financial Markets?

A financial market is not a single market. Rather, it is a collection of different markets, each specializing in a particular type of financial asset.

The most important financial markets include:

### Stock Market

The stock market is where ownership shares of publicly traded companies are bought and sold. By purchasing a share, an investor becomes a partial owner of the company and participates in its potential profits and losses.

---

### Foreign Exchange Market (Forex)

The **Foreign Exchange Market (Forex or FX)** is where one country's currency is exchanged for another, such as the euro against the U.S. dollar or the U.S. dollar against the Japanese yen.

The Forex market is the largest financial market in the world in terms of daily trading volume.

---

### Commodity Market

The commodity market is where raw materials and essential goods are traded.

Examples include:

* Gold and silver
* Crude oil
* Natural gas
* Copper and aluminum
* Wheat, corn, soybeans, and other agricultural products

Commodity prices are influenced by factors such as supply and demand, weather conditions, economic policies, and geopolitical events.

---

### Bond Market

The bond market is where debt securities issued by governments and corporations are traded. Investors who purchase bonds essentially lend money to the issuer in exchange for periodic interest payments and the return of the principal at maturity.

---

### Digital Asset Market

The digital asset market is where assets such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and other cryptocurrencies are bought and sold.

Compared with traditional financial markets, this market is relatively new and typically operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

---

### How Are Financial Markets Classified?

In addition to being categorized by the type of asset traded, financial markets can also be classified according to the type of contract being traded.

### Spot Market

In a **spot market**, transactions take place at the current market price, and ownership of the asset is usually transferred immediately or within a short settlement period.

This is the simplest and most common form of trading.

---

### Derivatives Market

In a **derivatives market**, the underlying asset itself is typically not traded. Instead, market participants trade contracts whose value is derived from another asset, such as a stock, currency, commodity, or cryptocurrency.

Futures contracts, options contracts, and other derivative instruments are among the most common products traded in these markets.

These markets and their instruments will be discussed in greater detail in later lessons.

---

### Are All Financial Markets the Same?

No. Each financial market has its own rules, trading hours, liquidity, level of risk, price volatility, and trading mechanisms.

For example, stock markets generally operate during fixed trading sessions, whereas the Forex market is open nearly 24 hours a day throughout the global business week. In contrast, cryptocurrency markets operate continuously, 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

Despite these differences, all financial markets are governed by the same fundamental principles of supply and demand, price discovery, and trade execution.

---

### Summary

A financial market is a collection of markets where financial assets are bought and sold between buyers and sellers. The stock market, foreign exchange market, commodity market, bond market, and digital asset market are among the most important financial markets, each with its own characteristics and trading rules. Financial markets can also be classified into spot markets and derivatives markets, which will be explored in detail in future lessons.

### One-sentence
A financial market is a marketplace where financial assets are traded between buyers and sellers, with prices determined by the forces of supply and demand.
